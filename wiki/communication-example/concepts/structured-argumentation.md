# Structured Argumentation

Structure an argument by introducing the problem, stating the thesis, presenting supporting reasons, addressing relevant counterarguments, and ending with a clear conclusion. Logical connectors make the relationships between claims explicit.

Arguments can draw on logical relationships (logos), speaker credibility (ethos), and audience-relevant emotional or practical considerations (pathos). The goal is a transparent case that can be understood and evaluated, not hidden manipulation.
